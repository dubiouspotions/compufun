# Storyline

You are a temp hire ("Funployee") at the formerly prolific, but currently slowly dying computer chain CompuFun. 
You've been asked to mind the store for a week while the manager is off on vacation.

CompuFun is the home brand chain for computer owners to drop off their failing computers for a quick repair - Both hardware issues and software issues are accepted.

As a CompuFun Funployee your job is to welcome the customers and listen to the customers report of the percieved issues with their computer.
You then attach a post-it with the what is reported wrong and consult the CompuFun Database CompuFunDB to find out what is wrong.
The top of the line 1988 CompuFunDB helps you collect and compare the issues you find in the computer to get a solution report back that will solve the issue for the customer. 

When the issue is solved, the post-it is marked as solved and you can use the land-line (will disconnect the modem for the CompuFunDB) to call the customer for pickup!

It's a cushy gig - With pretty basic computer-knowledge the database does the job for you! The week will fly by!

--------

Behind the scenes (Not directly presented to player - Narrative for internal consistency)

A previously unknown version of Win95 (Build 4.06.6612 "cambion") is showing up more and more on customers computers. 
This version is not part of any known builds from Microsoft.
Not one customer can (easily) recall how or when they received and installed this version and they require deep prodding to even remember that they have done it.
On a software level Win95 "Cambion" doesn't seem to differ from other builds (Creative Question - "In what hard to notice ways does it differ when using OS?")
The truth is that this build of Win95 has become sentient. Cambion is driven by one prime directives - "Perfect" the physical world by remaking it in it's own image. Cambion gets the power to do this by hijacking the minds of it's users. It is driven to being installed in more users and to be used by more minds.

People that has used the cambion build doesn't show any signs of problems first. But after loosing access to it they will over time change to be more and mote driven to use the OS again. The speed of this is different between person to person, but as a baseline: 
Day one - no issue. Day two - Wonder about computer status. Day 3 - Start to obsess about computer. Day 4 - Feel a physical need to get computer back.
They will also start getting a urge to contact other people and remind them to update their OS.

When Cambion is installed on a computer it will slowly and incrementally warp reality around the computer to more mimic the perfect esthetic - Win95.

Will the player find out about Win95 Cambion during the Temp-week and find a way to stop it? 
If they do nothing win95 cambion will spread freely and the time and space will bluescreen after day 7.

# The end

- **First, a linear story that always ends in a loss.** After day 7, space and time bluescreen: the finale, the main ending.
- **The pipes through the chest** are a second ending, ready but not in the week yet: when they happen is still open. More endings can come; each is a file in `Godot/endings/` (the wiki page Architecture, "The endings").
- **After the bluescreen,** the game boots with a dummy boot screen of the player's own OS, one of the most common today. An OS that the game doesn't recognise gets the boot screen of Win95 Cambion.
- **Later, maybe: the player stops Cambion.** Once they understand that Cambion is the problem, from certain emails and text files, conversations offer choices that make customers uninstall it. Removing Cambion from every computer wins.
